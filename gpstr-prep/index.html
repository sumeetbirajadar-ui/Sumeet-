What the exam is

GPSTR stands for Graduate Primary School Teacher Recruitment. The Karnataka Department of School Education uses it to hire teachers for Classes 6–8 in government schools. The post you applied for is the Mathematics & Science teacher (GPT). It is one combined post, so Paper 2 tests science as well as maths. Plan for both.

How selection works

  • A merit score is built from your exam marks plus your TET score, which has carried 20% weight in past rounds. Some sites say 2026 uses 70% exam + 20% TET + 10% academics, but that is not confirmed. Read the "selection" section of the PDF.
  • Seats are filled district by district, by category (GM, SC, ST, C1, 2A, 2B, 3A, 3B), with men/women and sub-quotas (Rural, Kannada medium, Project-displaced) and separate lists per medium.
  • A 1:2 list goes to document verification. Then a 1:1 provisional list, objections, and the final list.
  • There is a minimum mark in Papers 1 and 2. Reports put it around 35–50% depending on category, but check the notification. You will be aiming much higher anyway.

The strategy in five lines

  1. Paper 2 is your advantage. Aim for 120+/150. Descriptive maths rewards clean, step-by-step working and neat diagrams.
  2. Science is the risk. A maths graduate usually hasn't studied biology or chemistry since Class 10. That's why science gets 45 minutes every day.
  3. Ed. Psychology (50 marks) has the best return in Paper 1. The same 40–50 concepts come up every year. Study them daily until you can't get them wrong.
  4. Paper 3 is 100 marks for writing well. Write one essay or letter every week. Presentation matters.
  5. Past papers and mocks show you the real exam. The last 14 days are for full-length timed papers.

Daily routine, 3:30–7:30

Four hours, with the hardest work first while your mind is fresh. Keep the order fixed so you don't have to decide anything at 3:30 in the morning.

3:15–3:30
Wake, water, wash face. No phone. Open the plan to today's row.
3:30–4:40
Maths, 70 min. 25 min: read the topic from the KTBS/NCERT textbook and copy its formulas into your formula notebook. 45 min: solve 15–20 problems, including at least 2 written as full descriptive answers.
4:40–5:25
Science, 45 min. Read the textbook chapter and make one page of notes: definitions, SI units, one labelled diagram, key reactions. Finish with 10 MCQs.
5:25–5:35
Break. Walk, tea, stretch.
5:35–6:15
Paper 1: Educational Psychology, 40 min. One topic a day (see plan), then 15 KARTET/GPSTR past MCQs on it.
6:15–6:45
Paper 1 rotation, 30 min. Mon & Thu: Kannada grammar · Tue & Fri: English grammar · Wed: GK + current affairs · Sat: Computer + Health & Value.
6:45–7:15
Mixed MCQ set, 30 min. 30 questions from anything already studied, timed. This is spaced revision.
7:15–7:30
Error log. Write every wrong answer as "question → why wrong → correct idea". Re-read the log each test day.

Test day (every 7th day)

3:30–5:45
Timed test: 50 MCQs on the week's maths + science + Ed. Psych, then 3 descriptive maths questions.
5:45–6:45
Mark it strictly. Re-solve every wrong answer and add it to the error log.
6:45–7:30
Paper 3 writing. One essay (about 250 words) or formal letter in your medium language. Keep paragraphs neat, with a clear opening and conclusion.

Rules that protect the plan

  • Sleep by 9:30 pm. Waking at 3:15 on 5 hours of sleep will wear you out by week 3. Six hours is the minimum.
  • Missed a day? Don't try to double up. Continue from where you are and absorb the gap on the next test day.
  • Keep three notebooks: Formula, Error log, Ed. Psych one-liners. In the last week you revise only from these.
  • For any spare 5 minutes in the day (a bus or queue), carry the Ed. Psych one-liner notebook or the Formula sheet tab on your phone.

Proofs and constructions to practise for the descriptive part

  1. Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales) and its converse
  2. Pythagoras theorem and its converse
  3. Areas of similar triangles are in the ratio of squares of corresponding sides
  4. Tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius
  5. Lengths of tangents from an external point are equal
  6. Angle subtended at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference; angles in the same segment are equal
  7. Angle sum property of a triangle; exterior angle theorem
  8. Mid-point theorem
  9. Prove √2 (or √3, √5) is irrational
  10. Derive Sn of an AP; derive the quadratic formula
  11. Constructions: divide a segment in a given ratio · tangents from an external point · triangle similar to a given one with scale factor
  12. Graphical solution of a pair of linear equations

How to write a descriptive answer: Given → To prove → Construction → Proof with a reason for every step → Conclusion. Draw a neat labelled figure in pencil. Box the final answer and give units.

Maths teaching methods

  • Inductive: examples → rule (good for introducing). Deductive: rule → examples (good for practice).
  • Analytic: unknown → known (discovery). Synthetic: known → unknown (presentation). Used together.
  • Heuristic (Armstrong): the learner discovers. Project (Kilpatrick). Problem-solving. Laboratory method.
  • Concrete → Pictorial → Abstract. Manipulatives, maths lab, activity-based learning.
  • Error analysis, diagnostic testing → remedial teaching.

Assessment, policy and Karnataka programmes

  • CCE: formative (FA, during learning, to improve it) vs summative (SA, end of term). Tools: rubrics, portfolios, anecdotal records.
  • RTE Act 2009: free & compulsory education for ages 6–14; no detention/corporal punishment; 25% EWS seats in private schools; PTR 30:1 (primary), 35:1 (upper primary).
  • NCF 2005: connect learning to life outside school, move away from rote learning.
  • NEP 2020: 5+3+3+4 structure, foundational literacy & numeracy, mother tongue till Class 5 (preferably 8), competency-based assessment.
  • Karnataka: Nali-Kali (activity-based, Classes 1–3), Kalika Chetarike (learning recovery), Chinnara Angala, Samudayadatta Shale, SDMC.
  • Inclusive education: learning disabilities (dyslexia = reading, dyscalculia = maths, dysgraphia = writing), gifted, CWSN.

Books & sources

Keep the book list short: textbooks first, then past papers. Guides are for MCQ practice, not for learning a topic.

Paper 2 · Core

Karnataka State (KTBS) textbooks, Classes 6–10

Maths and Science, free PDFs at textbooksonline.karnataka.gov.in. The exam follows these books closely, and their diagrams and definitions are what examiners expect.

Add 1st PUC Maths chapters on Sets, Sequences & Series, Permutations & Combinations, Probability, and 2nd PUC Matrices & Determinants.

Paper 2 · Backup

NCERT Classes 6–10 Maths & Science

Free at ncert.nic.in. Use the exercises and examples for descriptive practice. Use RD Sharma / RS Aggarwal (Class 9–10) only for extra problems.

Paper 1 · Ed. Psych

One Ed. Psych book + KARTET papers

Your own D.El.Ed/B.Ed psychology notes plus one book (e.g. S.K. Mangal, Advanced Educational Psychology, or any Kannada Shaikshanika Manovijnana GPSTR/TET guide). Solve all KARTET Paper-II past papers. The psychology questions overlap heavily.

Paper 1 · Languages

Kannada & English grammar

Kannada: grammar sections of KTBS Class 8–10 first-language Kannada textbooks (ಸಂಧಿ, ಸಮಾಸ, ಅಲಂಕಾರ, ವಿಭಕ್ತಿ, ಕಾಲ). English: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar, chapters on tenses, voice, speech, articles, prepositions.

Paper 1 · GK

Karnataka-focused current affairs

Lucent's General Knowledge for static GK, plus one Kannada monthly (e.g. Spardha Vijetha or Spardha Spoorthi) for the last 6–8 months. Focus on Karnataka schemes, awards, sports, and the Constitution.
